# Chapter 2: What AI Changes — Intent

*Status: New chapter. No draft yet.*
*Intent set by: Nicole van der Hoeven | April 5, 2026*

## What this chapter argues

This is the chapter the 2024 book was missing. It addresses the elephant in the room — AI — so the rest of the book can proceed with honesty.

The argument: AI changes what learning in public *means*, but not whether it matters. In fact, it makes it matter more. This chapter establishes why.

## The core tension this chapter holds

AI can produce competent output. So why bother doing it yourself, publicly?

Because: doing it yourself, visibly, is how you prove there's a person behind the work. It's how you hold yourself accountable to actually thinking. It's how you resist the slow erosion of your own voice. And it's the only way to experience the genuine joy of making something — which AI cannot replicate.

## Key questions this chapter answers

1. If AI wrote your blog post, are you learning in public? (The authenticity question)
2. What's the difference between AI as amplifier and AI as replacement?
3. What does "using AI and not being used by it" actually look like in practice?
4. What does Nicole's own setup (Iris + Obsidian vault) demonstrate about this?
