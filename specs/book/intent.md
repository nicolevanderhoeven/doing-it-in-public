# Book Intent

*Set by Nicole van der Hoeven | April 5, 2026*

---

## The argument

Learning in public has never been more valuable than it is now — not despite AI, but because of it.

When AI can produce competent output on demand, the act of visibly, messily doing the work yourself becomes the most human thing you can do. This book is about how to use AI as a scaffold without letting it become a substitute for your own thinking.

**In one sentence:** AI can supplement your thinking, but it cannot — and should not — supplant it. Learning in public is how you make sure it doesn't.

---

## What this book is not

- Not an anti-AI argument. AI tools are genuinely useful and this book uses them.
- Not a hustle-culture book about building an audience. Learning in public is valuable whether or not anyone is watching.
- Not a tutorial for any specific tool. Tools change. The principles don't.

---

## What makes this book different

Three things only Nicole brings:

1. **The observability angle.** A DevOps/systems engineering lens on learning — instrumentation, signals, lead/lag indicators, making your process visible the way you'd make a distributed system observable. This runs through everything.

2. **The AI angle, lived honestly.** Nicole has a 13,000-note Obsidian vault, an AI assistant (Iris) who reads and synthesises it, and she's still the one doing the thinking. She's not theorising about AI-augmented learning — she's doing it.

3. **The non-hustle frame.** Learning in public because it makes you a better thinker, not because it builds your brand. The joy of making things yourself, even when AI could do it.

---

## Who this is for

Anyone doing knowledge work who is:
- Wondering what "authentic" contribution looks like when AI can generate content in their field
- Wanting to use AI tools without losing their own voice and thinking
- Curious about how to make their learning visible and meaningful — to themselves and others
- Drawn to the idea that there's genuine joy in the messy human process of learning

Not exclusively for tech workers, though the examples draw on tech. The principles apply to writers, researchers, designers, educators — anyone whose work is fundamentally about thinking and making.

---

## The feeling the reader should leave with

Energised. Not anxious about AI, but clearer about what they bring that AI doesn't. Equipped with concrete practices. And genuinely excited to go learn something in public.

---

## Central tension

The book holds two things in tension throughout:
- AI is a powerful tool that makes learning faster and more connective
- The act of learning yourself — slowly, messily, publicly — is irreplaceable and increasingly precious

It resolves this tension not by choosing a side, but by showing how to hold both at once.
