# Brainstorm: Book Pivot + Collaboration Structure

*Date: April 5, 2026*
*Participants: Nicole van der Hoeven, Iris (OpenClaw)*
*Type: Foundational — this conversation set the direction for the revised book*

---

## Context

Nicole had an existing outline for *Doing It in Public* written in 2024. She wanted to revisit it completely rather than just polish what existed. This conversation worked through whether the premise still holds, what's different now, and how to structure the collaboration going forward.

---

## Key decisions made

### 1. The book pivot

The 2024 outline was essentially *Show Your Work!* + PKM tiers. It lacked teeth. The 2026 version pivots to make AI central — not as a chapter, but as the reason the book needs to exist right now.

**New central thesis:** *Learning in public has never mattered more than it does in the age of AI. AI can supplement your thinking, but it cannot — and should not — supplant it.*

**Three essential features Nicole identified:**
- The observability angle (systems engineering lens on learning)
- The AI ethics angle (how to use AI and not be used by it)
- The non-hustle frame (learning in public because it makes you a better thinker, not to build a brand)

### 2. What makes this book different from existing books

Books reviewed/discussed:
- *Show Your Work!* by Austin Kleon (2014) — about sharing creative process, pre-GPT, doesn't answer "but what if AI did it?"
- swyx's "Learn in Public" essay (2019) — foundational for developers, no full book version exists
- *Building a Second Brain* by Tiago Forte — PKM without the public angle
- *Ultralearning* by Scott Young — intensive private learning, opposite pole

Nicole's differentiation: lived experience of AI-augmented PKM (13k-note vault + Iris), observability background, non-hustle philosophy, and the timely urgency of the AI question.

### 3. Structural question raised (unresolved)

The old Tiers structure (microlearning → systematising → longform → automating → community) was a complexity ladder. For a book making an argument, the structure should follow the logic of the argument. This is an open question.

Also unresolved: whether "Doing It in Public" remains the right title or whether the AI pivot wants a new name.

### 4. Collaboration structure

Nicole wanted to track and be transparent about the human-AI collaboration on the book. Decided on a combination of:

1. **Git commit conventions** — `[iris]`, `[nicole]`, `[collab]`, `[process]` tags on every commit
2. **Chapter provenance frontmatter** — each chapter file tracks who drafted and who revised
3. **Adapted OpenSpec** — `specs/` folder with intent (Nicole), design (Iris), and tasks per chapter

The process log (`process/`) captures key conversations like this one — published because the process is part of the book.
